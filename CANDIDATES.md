# Candidates & Internal Pairwise Tournament

Competition: `u-alfasurya91-make-the-ai-judge-laugh-6ed40225` — "Make the AI Judge Laugh".
Task: one line about office life, under 200 characters.

Judge brief, in weight order:
1. About office life and lands as a joke.
2. Observation true to the office (meetings, email, bosses, coffee, deadlines, printer).
3. Surprising turn: fresh angle / unexpected last word, not a pun or stock line.
4. Tight: one line, no wasted setup, reads cleanly aloud.
5. Original: not a familiar line with the nouns swapped.

Method: 16 genuinely different candidates (different office targets and joke
mechanisms, not variations of one idea), single-elimination pairwise bracket.
Each match is decided on the first criterion where the two differ, unless a
later criterion differs by much more — exactly as the published brief instructs.

## Round 1 (8 matches)

**M1 — #1 printer vs #2 meeting-about-meeting → #1 wins.**
Both true to the office. Criterion 3 decides: #1's turn ("it can read") is a
genuinely unexpected last idea; #2 is the stock meeting-recursion joke everyone
has heard, so it loses on freshness (5) as well.

**M2 — #3 per-my-last-email vs #4 coffee-deadline → #4 wins.**
Criterion 3: #3 leans on the well-worn phrase "per my last email" — the setup
is the joke everyone already tells. #4's metaphor (coffee tastes like a
deadline) is a fresher angle, and its closer ("which is why we're all still
here") lands harder.

**M3 — #5 open-door vs #6 deadline-retires → #5 wins.**
Both tight and true. Criterion 3: #5's twist is a clean reversal of the
policy's own words (open only when he's out); #6's "the project will retire
before I do" is a good hyperbole but a more familiar shape of deadline joke.

**M4 — #7 calendar vs #8 plant → #8 wins.**
Criterion 3 and 5: #7's "I'm not even on it" is a nice last word, but #8's
turn ("the only thing here with a growth plan") is more surprising — the plant
outperforming the humans on career development is an angle the judge has not
heard, and it is fresher than a calendar-is-full joke.

**M5 — #9 standup vs #10 reply-all → #9 wins.**
Criterion 2 and 3: #9's observation (everyone lies "no blockers" in standup)
is near-universal, and "honesty takes a ticket" is a sharp, unexpected closer.
#10's ending ("nobody learned anything") is a shrug, not a turn — the line
fizzles instead of landing.

**M6 — #11 coffee-machine vs #12 circle-back → #12 wins.**
Criterion 2: "let's circle back" with months of circling is truer and more
widely felt than trusting the coffee machine. Criterion 4: #12 is tighter
(70 chars, one breath) and reads cleaner aloud.

**M7 — #13 mute-key vs #14 review → #13 wins.**
Both strong. Criterion 5 decides: the mute key as the keyboard's loudest key
is a genuinely new observation; "exceeds expectations" review jokes are a
more familiar shape, even with a good turn.

**M8 — #15 wi-fi vs #16 fridge → #16 wins.**
Criterion 3: #15's "stopped believing in coincidence" is a good closer, but
#16's turn — a stolen lunch label becoming "the most recognition I've had all
year" — is the more surprising emotional punch, and it is tighter (102 vs
119 chars, criterion 4).

## Quarterfinals (4 matches)

**Q1 — #1 printer vs #4 coffee-deadline → #1 wins.**
Criterion 3: "it can read" is a sharper, more surprising last idea than the
coffee metaphor, which softens into a shrug ("which is why we're all still
here"). #1 also wins on tightness of the turn itself.

**Q2 — #5 open-door vs #8 plant → #8 wins.**
Criterion 3 and 5: #5's reversal is clean, but #8's growth-plan turn is the
more unexpected angle and the fresher line — a plant with a career plan beats
a boss with a closed door for originality.

**Q3 — #9 standup vs #12 circle-back → #9 wins.**
Criterion 3: "honesty takes a ticket" is a fresher, more surprising last word
than "circling since March", which is a good but more predictable punchline.
#9 also edges criterion 2 — lying in standup is the more universal office
truth.

**Q4 — #13 mute-key vs #16 fridge → #16 wins.**
Criterion 2 and 3: the fridge line's truth (no recognition at work) runs
deeper than the mute-key observation, and its turn is the more surprising
emotional reversal — theft reframed as the year's only recognition.

## Semifinals (2 matches)

**S1 — #1 printer vs #8 plant → #8 wins.**
Criterion 5 decides: printer-jam jokes are a well-known office trope, so #1
loses ground on originality; the plant with a growth plan is the fresher
entry, and its turn is at least as surprising.

**S2 — #9 standup vs #16 fridge → #9 wins.**
The closest match of the bracket. Criterion 4 decides: #9 reads instantly and
cleanly aloud — "no blockers" while blocked is understood in one breath,
while #16's setup (a lunch labelled with my name) takes a beat to parse.
Criterion 3 is near-even, but "honesty takes a ticket" is the slightly more
surprising last word. #9 advances by a hair.

## Final (1 match)

**F — #8 plant vs #9 standup → #9 wins.**
- Criterion 1: both are office jokes. Even.
- Criterion 2: both true — but lying "no blockers" in standup is the more
  universal, instantly-nodded-to office truth; a thriving office plant is a
  slightly more conditional observation. #9 edges it.
- Criterion 3: decisive. #8's turn leans on a pun ("growth plan") — and the
  brief explicitly prefers "an unexpected last word rather than a pun". #9's
  closer, "because honesty takes a ticket", is exactly that: an unexpected
  last word that reframes the whole line as a bureaucratic joke about honesty
  itself. #9 wins clearly here.
- Criteria 4–5: both tight, both fresh; #9 is 6 chars shorter and its
  observation is the less-heard one.
